# Apple Ads: a small learning test, not a scale-up

Prepared September 17, 2026. Proposed setup only; budget and activation require the owner's approval.

## Recommendation

Start with **up to $100 in total ad spend over ten days**, preferably using Apple's eligible new-advertiser credit. Do not start a recurring, open-ended campaign. If credit is absent, my recommendation is a smaller **$50 / five-day test**, subject to approval. Neither amount is guaranteed to produce meaningful volume or profitable customers.

Cake Club costs $0.99 in the US with no additional purchases. An assumed 30% commission leaves about $0.69 per sale; enrollment in Apple's Small Business Program would reduce commission to 15%, leaving about $0.84. Taxes, refunds, and other adjustments can reduce those proceeds. Enrollment is unverified. Acquisition above actual net proceeds cannot pay for itself through that sale alone. [Apple Small Business Program](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/)

AppTweak reports a US Games median Search Results CPI of $12.28 and CPT of $4.21, using a 2025 dataset covering roughly 3,500 apps and 50,000 campaigns. This broad commercial sample is not a forecast for a paid indie cake game. At that CPI, $100 would buy only about eight installs, not enough for a reliable profitability conclusion. [AppTweak benchmarks](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/apple-ads-benchmarks)

The purpose is to find whether a narrow, affordable search niche exists and learn what converts. Organic player feedback is a better use of time than scaling obviously unprofitable acquisition.

## Starting exact-match keywords

cake game; cake games; cake decorating; cake decorating game; cake maker; cake maker game; cake stacking; baking game; baking games; frosting game.

These are relevance-based candidates; search volume and auction competitiveness are unverified. Avoid broad terms such as “games” initially. Keep competitor names out of this first test. If available, exclude irrelevant intent such as real cake delivery, bakery ordering, cake recipes, and free games; review actual search terms before expanding negatives.

## Guardrails and measurement

1. Confirm app discoverability and live product-page identity. Apple says newly published apps may take up to 24 hours to appear. If still missing after that, check the linked App Store Connect account and roles before rebuilding anything. [Apple setup troubleshooting](https://ads.apple.com/app-store/help/get-started/0052-solve-setup-and-access-issues)
2. Check Billing → Promo Credit. Apple's $100 credit has eligibility and account-linking requirements. Do not assume it was granted; record its balance and any displayed conditions before starting. [Apple promo credit](https://ads.apple.com/app-store/help/billing/0032-apple-ads-promo-credit)
3. Record impressions, taps, spend, new downloads, search terms, and product-page conversion daily. Separate new downloads from redownloads. Low-volume reporting may be delayed or suppressed.
4. Pause clearly irrelevant terms. Review at $10 cumulative spend before continuing; the first checkpoint may arrive sooner than a day because the daily budget is averaged.
5. With zero new downloads after $10, pause and inspect relevance and the store page; do not keep buying traffic without a diagnosis. Any deliberate restart stays inside the approved test cap.
6. If there are few impressions at $0.20 CPT, classify the test as insufficient delivery, not proof of no demand. Do not increase bids automatically. At 25% tap-to-purchase conversion, $0.20 CPT implies $0.80 CPI; at 10%, it implies $2.00. Those are scenarios, not forecasts.
7. Do not scale unless observed acquisition cost is below actual net proceeds and the sample is credible. Promo credit reduces cash cost of the experiment, not the recurring cost of acquiring a buyer.
8. Review organic sales alongside the test, but do not attribute every download or ranking change to ads. One small campaign cannot establish causality.
